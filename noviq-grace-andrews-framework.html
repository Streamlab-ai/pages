02 — The Mindset

You're a media company
that sells a product.

People are allergic to sales messaging. They don't come to social media to be sold to — they come for relief. Stop leading with logos. Lead with entertainment that solves their real problems.

Do This
Episodic, addictive content
Build series. Make people want to come back. Think like a TV producer, not a marketer.
Avoid This
Logo-first, feature-first
Pushing your logo and product features in every post. That's what makes people scroll.
The Goal
Minutes with your brand
Get someone to spend minutes — not seconds — consuming your content. That's where trust begins.
NOVIQ application — Don't post "NOVIQ manages your STR." Post the problem: the landlord who self-managed and burned out. The guest who couldn't get a refund. The owner who didn't know about dynamic pricing. Make them feel seen — then offer the solution.
03 — The Funnel

Attention → Connection → Trust.

The old linear funnel is dead. Discovery is non-linear — a clip, an algorithm rec, a WhatsApp forward. Your job is to maximize touchpoints across every surface, not control the path.

1
Attention
Stop the scroll. Get in front of the right people, across multiple platforms, repeatedly. Short-form clips, trends, hooks. This is pure reach — don't expect trust yet.
↓
2
Connection
They see you again. And again. They resonate with the content. They convert to a follow. They start sharing with their network. Familiarity is building.
↓
3
Trust
Built over time through multiple touchpoints that offer real value. At this stage, purchase feels inevitable. They don't think about it — they just do it.

05 — Measurement

Know what you're measuring
before you post.

Define success criteria before a piece goes live. If you don't, you'll get distracted by whatever Instagram tells you worked — which may be completely unrelated to your actual goal.

"If you don't know what you're measuring, you don't know why you're creating the content."
✓ Signal
Share rate
People sending it to each other's DMs. They're having a conversation about it. Highest trust signal in social.
✓ Signal
Comment quality
Not emojis. Actual responses from people who watched and engaged with the idea. Drives conversation.
✓ Signal
Inbound DMs / inquiries
Direct business outcomes. Someone saw the post and took action. The only metric that definitely ties to revenue.
↓ Noise
View count
Forgettable seconds. A million views where no one can recall a single thing. Useful only if reach is the stated goal.
↓ Noise
Follower spike
Feels like a win. But follows you didn't earn through connection drop off fast. High follow = low retention.
↓ Noise
Likes
The most passive action available. Tells you very little about intent or business alignment. Check, don't chase.
06 — Format Strategy

Memorable minutes vs.
forgettable seconds.

Short-Form Only
Forgettable seconds.
A million views where no one recalls a single thing. It takes months — maybe years — of short-form clips before someone builds real trust with you. Fast exposure, slow relationship.
Long-Form Investment
Memorable minutes.
One hour on a podcast can move someone from zero awareness to full trust in a single session. You can't achieve this with short-form alone. If conversion is the goal, long-form must be in the mix.
The jar analogy: Think of trust as a jar you're trying to fill. Every time someone watches a short-form clip, a few drops go in. Every time they spend an hour with your long-form content, the jar fills fast. Build both — but don't expect the jar to fill from reels alone.
07 — Audience

Speak to someone specific,
or speak to no one.

The algorithm is not working against you. It's trying to figure out who this content is for so it can serve it to them. Make its job easy. If your content is unclear about who it's for, you reach no one.

01
Build a granular picture
Morning routine. Group chats. Spending habits. Where they hang out online. "20–40 year olds" is not a brief — it's an excuse to speak to no one.
02
Go where they already are
Facebook groups. Reddit. Slack communities. Sit with your audience before you speak to them. Listen to their actual problems. Feed those back as content.
03
Check your demographics
Look at the back end. Are the people you think you're speaking to actually watching? If not — adjust the content, or adjust the audience.
04
Be willing to pivot
The best founders remove ego and do what's best for the audience. You may think you know who it's for. The data will tell you. Listen to it.
08 — Virality

Virality is a sugar rush.

Big views.
Empty jar.
You can engineer a viral hit. There is a science to it. But a viral post gets you in front of a lot of people you haven't bought in. Then the come-down. They forget you. You start over.
  • Virality = exposure without relationship
  • The come-down is real — where did everyone go?
  • Goal: repeated exposure to the right people over time
  • Always ask before posting: for what? How do we measure success?
  • Content for business outcomes — not views
  • The point of going viral is to keep doing it — so they see you multiple times
