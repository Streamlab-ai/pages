Offer architecture

From information to implementation.

The competitive idea is not “more leadership training.” It is a structured rhythm that helps behavior survive contact with real work.

The problem

The learning ends. Old patterns return.

People leave workshops informed and inspired, then pressure restores the behavior the organization meant to change.

The promise

Intentional practice people can see.

Evidence of Design creates a paced process for attention, observation, action, and reflection across real work.

Soak InGive one idea focused attention.
ObserveNotice behavior and conditions at work.
ImplementPractice one specific behavior.
LookReflect, adapt, and decide what continues.
Approval checkpoint: Confirm the exact meaning of the final “L”—Look Back, Look Forward, or both. The strategy is valid either way; the published language waits for approval.

Measurement

Measure movement, not noise.

Views and followers can support awareness, but they do not define launch success. The campaign is accountable to room quality and pipeline progression.

DEMAND

Right people enter

Scorecard downloads, waitlist growth, source, registrations, room-capacity fill, and role mix.

EXPERIENCE

People return and advocate

Attendance, repeat attendance, structured feedback, referrals, and approved proof.

REVENUE

Organizations progress

Qualified conversations, meetings, next activities, opportunities, proposals, pipeline, and revenue influenced.

Primary launch question: Are the right people moving from experience to an owned organizational next step?
Baseline rule: Use the first two sessions to establish conversion baselines before inventing percentage targets. The immediate standard is full rooms with the right buyer/champion mix and complete follow-up.
